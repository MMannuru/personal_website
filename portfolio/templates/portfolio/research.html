Inverse Design of Non-linear Mechanical Metamaterials with Generative AI

Metamaterials are human engineered structures that exhibit extraordinary properties, such as the ability to bend light or absorb sound, which are impossible with regular materials. Their unprecedented properties come from the careful arrangement of their internal structure rather than the material they are composed of. They hold the potential to revolutionize fields like advanced manufacturing, aerospace, and robotics. However, designing these materials is a highly complex process due to their intricate geometries and the nonlinear mechanical behaviors they exhibit under various conditions.

Traditional deep learning methods fall short when it comes to predicting these complex behaviors because they often rely on simplified stress-strain data, which do not fully capture the intricate deformation patterns within the material.

Our goal is to tackle this challenge by training generative models on detailed internal stresses and displacement fields. This enables our models to learn deep, underlying non-linear mechanical relationship and predict microstructures with unique properties.

Automating 3D Point Cloud Object Classification Algorithms

What is point cloud classification?

Point cloud object classification is the process of identifying and labeling 3D objects from a set of points in space. These points are typically generated by sensors like LiDAR. Unlike traditional ML/DL methods that work on structured data like images or text, point clouds are unstructured, irregular, and often sparse. This makes their classification more challenging, as models need to capture both the spatial geometry and the underlying relationships between points.

Traditional approaches use algorithms like PointNet++ and ModelNet, but optimizing these models often involves tedious trial-and-error with limited flexibility, as they rely on rigid rules for tuning parameters. Recently techniques like Cartesian genetic programming have been explored, but models evolve on pre-set rules without much flexibility or creativity.

We want to use large language model (LLM) guided evolution to dynamically generate mutations and crossovers in the model's architecture. With our approach, we want to explore a vast design space to improve the accuracy and efficiency of existing algorithms.

Exploring the Genetic Basis of Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD)

In high school, I was fortunate enough to work in a biomedical research lab at Emory University. This was very impactful because it was my first real-world experience.

My research was very bioinformatics focused. I spent a lot of time analyzing large molecular datasets to explore why there are so many genes implicated with in development of autism and the types of phenotypes that arise as a result of their knockout. I was also very interested in "ASD genes" that are involved in epigenetic modifiction (processes that alter gene expression without changing the actual DNA sequence). I discovered genes that are essential for human development and growth. I also revealed 2 non-neural physiological processes that are common among ASD genes and may serve as biomarkers that could aid ASD diagnosis/treatment. These pathways and processes can also be studied to identify drug targets that inhibit the epigenetic modifications behind these mechanisms to aid future treatments.

Title: Systematic Analysis of the Epigenetic Machinery Linked to Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD)

Abstract: Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) is a complex neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by deficits in social interaction, communication, and repetitive behaviors. Extensive genetic research has revealed evidence of hundreds of genes being implicated in ASD development. These genes have been found to be involved in specific cellular processes, such as synaptic function, Wnt signaling, and epigenetic modification. While the roles of some of these genes in brain development are clear, others remain poorly understood. Epigenetic modification involves the addition or removal of chemical marks on DNA (or associated proteins) that can alter gene expression without actually changing the underlying DNA sequence. Over 20% of the genes implicated in ASD development are involved in epigenetic modification and their precise role in ASD development is not yet fully understood. In this study, I used bioinformatic analysis in R to identify the shared functional consequences of ASD genes involved in epigenetic modification. I studied a total of 8,368 genes, investigated all 822 unique phenotypes possible from a gene knockout, and analyzed 66,000+ genotype-phenotype associations to identify the key developmental processes and pathways behind these genes, with the aim of understanding their functional consequences in ASD development. My results implicate that increased lean body mass, embryonic lethality prior to organogenesis, and preweaning lethality (incomplete penetrance) are the developmental pathways/processes underlying ASD etiology, and can serve as potential epigenetic ASD biomarkers to help aid future diagnosis. These pathways and processes can also be studied to identify drug targets that inhibit the epigenetic modifications behind these mechanisms to aid future treatments.
